EV charger installation in Port Dover.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Port Dover property

Collect the charger box, product sheet and receipt, and photograph the rating label from the outside. Sketch the route from the parking spot to the panel, noting whether it crosses a finished basement ceiling or wall.

Ask the contractor whether the charger you already own can be used as it is, and what paperwork will show insurers or lenders the equipment and its circuit were installed and notified to ESA.

What should the assessment establish?

Is the charger that a Port Dover household already owns approved equipment, and does it suit the spot where it will be mounted? If the unit proves unsuitable, the scope says so plainly and explains the alternative. Can the property supply serve the added load, or is another design needed? The contractor's finding on the supply sits in the scope beside any other design proposed. Described in plain words are the dedicated circuit, the cable route and any outdoor-rated parts. A lender or insurer may ask for both the scope and the product sheet, so keep the two together.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I keep a charger I bought before the assessment?

Possibly, but that is for the contractor to judge once the equipment and the supply are reviewed. Send the product sheet with the enquiry, and expect the written scope to say whether the unit is suitable.

What will an insurer want to know about a home charger circuit?

Only the insurer can confirm that. What you can hold ready is a short file: the product sheet, the contractor's written description of the dedicated circuit and route, and the ESA notification record.
