An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Port Dover property

Photograph each receptacle face from straight on, note which rooms have two-prong or loose ones, and write down what is plugged into each. Find any earlier invoice or inspection note that mentions them.

Ask the contractor what each worn or two-prong receptacle is connected to, whether replacing the device alone answers the concern, and what written note will show a lender or insurer what was done.

What should the assessment establish?

The homeowner decides whether to go ahead with any work on worn, loose or two-prong receptacles in a Port Dover house, and when, using the written scope. For each receptacle reviewed, the scope records what it is wired to and whether the wiring behind it can accept a replacement device. The contractor keeps devices needing only replacement apart from locations needing further work. Any circuit that also feeds a finished basement room, where walls limit access, is noted. Every receptacle reviewed is listed with its outcome, which gives insurers or lenders a plain record, not a general statement.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does an old two-prong outlet always call for new wiring?

That depends on what the contractor finds behind the device, so it cannot be answered from the outlet face alone. Ask what the written scope says about each location, including any that stay as they are.

What should I tell an insurer about loose or worn outlets?

Describe what you can see and where, without guessing at causes, and say whether you have arranged an assessment. Ask the insurer whether it wants a contractor's written findings once they exist.
