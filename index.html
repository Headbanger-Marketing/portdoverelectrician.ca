Before work begins

Match the written record to what the request asks.

An unfinished basement may leave the panel and cables in view. By contrast, a bungalow or split-level with a finished or part-finished basement may have its panel inside a finished room or behind storage, and cables running through ceilings and walls now closed in.

An insurer or lender may ask what wiring, panel or earlier work the property has. Gather any inspection reports, invoices and ESA paperwork first, then ask the insurer or lender exactly which written record it will accept.

What records can answer an insurer's question about my wiring?

Useful records include earlier ESA paperwork, invoices for past electrical work, inspection reports and clear photographs of panel labels. The insurer can say which of these it will accept, because the request itself sets the required form.

Should I finish the basement ceiling before a lender reviews the wiring?

Check with the lender first, since a closed ceiling hides cable routes, and junction boxes must stay accessible. Ask the contractor to document what is visible now, so the written record is not lost when the finishing is done.

Which parts of a quote matter when an insurer asks for paperwork?

Compare what each quote says it will inspect, record and report, not only the work it lists. A quote that names the equipment reviewed and the written result is easier to give to an insurer.

Who needs to agree before a contractor visits for a lender request?

The person who owns the property or can authorize work should agree, and a tenant or co-owner may need to allow access. Name that person in the enquiry so the contractor knows who approves the scope.

If an insurer is waiting on me, does the enquiry set up an appointment?

No. Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you may hire agrees with you directly on the dates and on what the work includes and costs. It confirms its business details and licence, which you can check through ESA. The site informs, and reviews the enquiries it receives, without arranging visits or taking part in pricing or wiring.
